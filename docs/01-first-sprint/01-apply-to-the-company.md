## How Hiring Actually Works

A typical software hiring process has most of these stages, although there is a TON of variety:

1. **You apply.** You submit a resume and cover letter, and the company decides whether to look closer.
2. **Application and resume screen.** A recruiter (or a filter program) decides in under a minute whether your resume earns a closer look.
3. **Phone screen.** A short conversation confirming you are a real person with the background your resume claims.
4. **Technical evaluation.** Some combination of coding interviews, take-home projects, and work samples, where you demonstrate skills instead of describing them.
5. **Team interviews.** Conversations about collaboration, judgment, and whether people want to work with you.
6. **Offer and negotiation.**
7. ...
8. **Profit.** You are now an employee, and you will hopefully make money.

This course simulates the parts you can practice: the resume (now, and again in chapter 3), the work sample (the whole first half), the technical interview (the midterm), and the cover letter (chapter 5). Employers increasingly favor work samples and structured interviews because they measure the job instead of measuring interview trivia. That is good news for you: the way to prepare for them is to get good at the actual work.

## Resume v0

Your second assignment is a first draft of your resume, which the course calls **Resume v0**. You will revise it into Resume v1 in chapter 3, after you have a few weeks of work sample experience to describe.

If you already have a resume, you can submit it unchanged. If not, then right now the goal is an ugly one-page draft that you spend as little time as possible on.

- **Keep it to one page.** Recruiters spend seconds on the first pass. One page, readable at a glance.
- **Lead with education and projects.** As a student, your coursework and projects are your experience. Name real technologies and real things you built.
- **Describe outcomes, not duties.** "Built a four-player card game in TypeScript with a test suite" beats "was responsible for programming."
- **Do not pad it.** Empty space is better than filler, and interviewers ask about whatever you list. Every line should be something you are prepared to discuss.
- **Nothing from High School.** Your resume is about your professional experience, not your childhood.

Submit Resume v0 on Canvas. Don't waste time polishing it now: you just need to submit SOMETHING.
